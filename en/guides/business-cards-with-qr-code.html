What is a vCard?

A vCard (Virtual Contact File) is an international standard for exchanging contact information. The format is used by all major platforms — iOS, Android, Windows and macOS — and supports fields such as:

  • Full name (first name and last name)
  • Organization and job title
  • Phone numbers (mobile, work, personal)
  • Email addresses
  • Website URL
  • Physical address

When a vCard is encoded into a QR code, any smartphone can read and interpret the data automatically. Most camera apps on modern phones recognize the vCard format without needing a separate app. The result is that the recipient gets a popup with the option to save the contact directly.

Design tips for QR codes on business cards

A QR code on a business card needs to be both functional and aesthetic. Here are the most important design principles:

Contrast is crucial

The QR code must have high contrast against the background. Black on white is the standard, but you can use dark brand colors on a light background. Avoid light colors like yellow or light gray for the QR modules themselves.

Preserve the "quiet zone"

There must be at least 4 modules' width of empty space (quiet zone) around the QR code. Without this zone, scanners may have difficulty recognizing the code's boundaries.

Add a call-to-action

Write "Scan me" or "Save contact" below the QR code. Many people don't yet automatically know what to do with a QR code on a business card.

Place the code strategically

The back of the business card is the most popular choice. Alternatively, the QR code can be placed in a corner on the front so it doesn't dominate the design.

Size and placement of the QR code

The size of the QR code is critical for reliable scanning. Here are guidelines for business cards:

  • Minimum 2 x 2 cm: For a standard business card (85 x 55 mm), we recommend a QR code of at least 20 x 20 mm. Larger is better.
  • Optimal size: 2.5-3 cm: This size provides good scannability, even with older phones or in poor lighting.
  • Placement on the back: This gives the most space for the QR code and keeps the front clean. Add the text "Scan for contact info" as a prompt.
  • Placement on the front: Choose a corner (typically bottom right) and keep the QR code compact. Make sure it doesn't overlap with text or logo.

Remember: The more data the QR code contains, the denser the modules become. If you include all contact fields plus address, the code becomes more complex and requires a larger physical size. Consider only including the most important fields.

Error correction: Important for printed business cards

QR codes have a built-in error correction mechanism based on the Reed-Solomon algorithm. This means that even a partially damaged or smudged code can still be read. There are four levels:

  1. L (Low, ~7%): Minimal error correction. Produces the smallest and simplest QR code. Best for digital surfaces where the code won't wear.
  2. M (Medium, ~15%): The standard choice and a good balance between size and reliability. Fine for most business cards.
  3. Q (Quartile, ~25%): Recommended for printed business cards. Tolerates scratches, fingerprints and light wear from a pocket or wallet.
  4. H (High, ~30%): Highest error correction. Necessary if you want to place a logo over the QR code, or if the card is used in harsh conditions.

For printed business cards, we recommend level Q or H. A business card is often stored in a pocket or wallet, and the code can become slightly damaged over time. With high error correction, you ensure the code can still be scanned even after some wear.

Networking in practice: How to use your QR business card

Having a QR code on your business card is only half the story — the other half is using it effectively. Here are concrete situations where the QR business card makes a difference:

Conferences and trade shows

At a busy conference, you meet many people in a short time. Instead of handing out stacks of business cards, you can show the QR code on your physical card or directly on your phone. The recipient scans and has your details immediately.

Sales meetings

After a sales meeting, you want to be easy to contact. Give your business card with QR code and briefly explain that the client just needs to scan to save your details. It removes friction from the follow-up.

Informal meetings

You meet a potential business partner at a dinner or event. Instead of searching for paper and a pen, you show the QR code on your phone. Elegant and fast.

Email signature and LinkedIn

Add the QR code to your email signature or LinkedIn background image. That way people can save your details even when you're not in the same room.

A good tip is to save the QR code as an image on your phone, so you can always show it — even when you don't have physical business cards on hand. That way you're always ready to network.

Frequently asked questions about business cards with QR code

Can all smartphones scan a vCard QR code?

Yes, both iPhone (iOS 11 and newer) and Android phones (version 8 and newer) can scan vCard QR codes directly with the camera app. There is no need to install a separate app. Older phones can use a free QR scanner from the app store.

Which file format is best for printing on business cards?

We recommend SVG format for printing. SVG is vector-based and can be scaled to any size without loss of quality. Alternatively, you can use PNG in high resolution (at least 1024 x 1024 pixels). Avoid JPG, as compression can make the modules blurry.

Can the QR code use my company's colors?

Yes, you can freely choose the color of the QR code modules. The important thing is that the color is dark enough to create high contrast against a light background. Dark blue, dark green or burgundy work well. Avoid light or pastel-colored QR modules.

Does the QR code on my business card expire?

No, a vCard QR code never expires. The data is encoded directly in the QR code's patterns, so it works independently of servers or internet connections. As long as the code is physically intact and can be scanned, it will always work.
