Why restaurants use QR codes

Since 2020, QR codes have become a permanent part of the restaurant experience. What started as a hygiene measure during the pandemic has proven to have lasting benefits for both restaurant owners and guests. Today, the majority of restaurants, cafes and bars use QR codes for at least one purpose in their daily operations.

The benefits are many and concrete. Restaurants that have implemented QR-based menus typically report:

Lower printing costs

Digital menus eliminate the need to print new menus for price changes, seasonal menus or daily specials. An average restaurant can save thousands annually.

Faster updates

When a dish is sold out, or you want to highlight a new specialty, you can update the digital menu in real time. Guests always see the current selection.

Better customer experience

Guests can browse the menu at their own pace, view photos of dishes and read about allergens and ingredients. This leads to more informed ordering.

Efficient operations

With QR-based ordering, guests can place orders directly from the table. This reduces wait times and frees up staff to focus on service and quality.

Digital menus with QR codes

The most widespread use case for QR codes in restaurants is the digital menu. A QR code on the table links the guest to a webpage with the full menu, and the guest can read it on their own phone.

There are several approaches to digital menus, and the right solution depends on your restaurant's size, budget and needs:

  • Simple PDF menu: The easiest solution is to upload a PDF of your menu and generate a QR code that links directly to the file. Ideal for smaller restaurants that don't change the menu often.
  • Responsive webpage: A dedicated menu page optimized for mobile provides the best experience. You can add images, allergen info and descriptions. The code can be generated for free with QR Tool.
  • Integrated ordering system: Advanced solutions let guests order and pay directly via the QR code. This requires integration with a POS system but significantly improves efficiency.
  • Multilingual menus: With a digital menu, you can easily offer the menu in multiple languages. Perfect for tourist areas and international restaurants.

Regardless of which solution you choose, the menu should always be mobile-friendly. Over 90% of guests will open the link on a smartphone, so the text must be readable without zooming, and navigation must work with touch.

WiFi QR codes for guests

Free WiFi is one of the most requested services among restaurant guests. A WiFi QR code makes it effortless for guests to get online, without staff having to dictate long passwords.

Here's how it works in practice: You generate a QR code that contains your WiFi network name and password. When the guest scans the code with their smartphone, the phone automatically connects to the network. No manual entry, no errors, no questions for the waiter.

Tips for WiFi in restaurants:

  • Set up a separate guest network isolated from your operations network, so guests cannot access the point-of-sale system or internal devices.
  • Limit the speed on the guest network so it doesn't affect your POS terminals and other business-critical systems.
  • Place the QR code visually on each table, at the entrance or on the menu. Add the text "Scan for free WiFi" so guests know what the code does.
  • Update the code when you change the WiFi password. Use QR Tool to quickly generate a new code.

Ordering and payment via QR

QR-based ordering and payment is the next evolution in restaurant operations. The guest scans a QR code on the table, browses the menu, places their order and pays — all from their phone.

This model has several advantages for restaurant operations:

Reduced wait time

Guests don't need to wait for a waiter to order. They can place orders at any time, which improves the overall experience and increases table turnover.

Fewer errors

When guests enter their own order, the risk of misunderstandings is minimized. The order is sent directly to the kitchen in the correct format.

Upselling

Digital menus can automatically suggest sides, desserts or beverages. Restaurants with QR ordering often see 10-15% higher average order value.

Contactless payment

Guests can pay via mobile payment, credit card or other digital payment methods directly from their phone. No waiting for the bill.

Implementing QR ordering requires integration with your POS system. Most modern point-of-sale systems support this functionality.

Feedback and reviews

Online reviews are crucial for restaurants. A QR code that links directly to your Google review page or Trustpilot profile makes it easy for satisfied guests to leave a positive review while the experience is still fresh.

Place a review QR code strategically:

  • At the exit: Guests on their way out have just finished their experience and are most likely to leave a review.
  • On the receipt: Add a small QR code on the receipt with the text "Enjoyed your experience? Leave a review."
  • On table displays: A discreet QR code on the table with a friendly invitation to share the experience.

You can also use QR codes for internal feedback. Link to a short survey (e.g., via Google Forms) to collect guests' opinions on the food, service and atmosphere, without it being public. This gives you valuable insight you can act on.

Practical tips for implementation

A successful implementation of QR codes in your restaurant requires more than just printing a code and placing it on the table. Here are the most important considerations:

  • Print in high quality: Use SVG or high-resolution PNG format (at least 1024px) when generating your QR codes. Download from QR Tool in the format that best suits your needs.
  • Lamination: QR codes on tables are exposed to spills, cleaning agents and wear. Lamination significantly extends durability.
  • Contrast and size: Use a dark QR code on a light background. The code should be at least 3x3 cm for easy scanning. Test with several different phones.
  • Context text: Always write what the QR code does. "Scan for menu", "Scan for WiFi" or "Order here" are short, clear instructions.
  • Backup plan: Always have physical menus available for guests who cannot or do not want to use QR codes. Not everyone is comfortable with the technology.
  • Regular testing: Check your QR codes weekly. Links can change, menu pages can go down, and physical codes can become damaged.
  • Branding: Customize the QR code colors to match your restaurant's visual identity. With QR Tool you can choose colors that match your brand.

Step-by-step: Get started with QR codes in your restaurant

Follow these steps to implement QR codes in your restaurant from scratch:

  1. Identify your needs: Start by deciding what you want to use QR codes for. The menu is the obvious starting point, but also consider WiFi, ordering and feedback.
  2. Create your digital content: Build a mobile-friendly menu page, configure your guest WiFi, or create a feedback form. Make sure the content is ready before you generate codes.
  3. Generate your QR codes: Use QR Tool to create professional QR codes. Choose colors that match your brand, and download in SVG format for the best print quality.
  4. Design table displays: Create attractive displays or stickers with the QR code, your restaurant's logo and a short instruction. Laminate them for durability.
  5. Place strategically: Put the QR codes on every table, at the entrance and possibly in the window. Make sure they are visible and easily accessible.
  6. Train the staff: Ensure all employees know what the QR codes do and can help guests who have questions. Staff should also be able to offer alternatives.
  7. Test thoroughly: Scan all codes with different phones (iPhone and Android) and verify that everything works correctly. Test in different lighting conditions.
  8. Collect feedback: After implementation, ask guests and staff about their experience. Adjust and improve continuously based on feedback.

Frequently asked questions about QR codes in restaurants

Does it cost anything to create QR codes for my restaurant?

No, you can create QR codes completely free with QR Tool. There are no hidden costs, no subscriptions and no limit on the number of codes. You only pay for any printing and lamination.

What if my menu changes frequently?

If your menu changes daily, you should use a QR code that links to a fixed URL where you update the content. That way you don't need to generate new QR codes — just update the webpage. The QR code itself stays the same.

Do QR codes work for all guests?

Most smartphones from 2018 onwards can scan QR codes directly with the camera app. However, it's important to have physical menus as backup for guests who prefer them or who have older phones.

How large should the QR code be on the table?

A QR code on a restaurant table should be at least 4x4 cm for easy scanning. Remember to include a white border (quiet zone) around the code. If guests scan from a standing position, the code should be even larger — at least 6x6 cm.

Can I use QR codes for takeaway and delivery?

Yes, QR codes are ideal for takeaway. Print a code on the packaging that links to your online ordering page, your menu or a feedback form. It creates a connection with the guest even after the visit.
