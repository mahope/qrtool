Modernize your event with QR codes

QR codes have become an indispensable tool for event organizers. From ticket sales and access control to WiFi sharing and feedback collection — learn how to use QR codes to create better experiences for your event guests.

Why events use QR codes

The event industry has undergone a digital transformation in recent years, and QR codes play a central role in this development. Whether you're organizing a conference with 500 attendees, a wedding with 100 guests or a festival with thousands of visitors, QR codes can simplify almost every aspect of your event.

The biggest advantage of QR codes for events is that they bridge the physical and digital worlds. A guest can scan a code on a poster and instantly access the program, sign up for a workshop or share their contact details. It saves time for both organizers and attendees, reduces paper consumption and gives a more professional impression.

Faster check-in

QR codes on tickets make entrance faster and significantly reduce queues. A scanner can verify a ticket in under one second.

Reduced paper consumption

Digital tickets, programs and maps replace printed materials. It's both cheaper and more sustainable for your event.

Better guest experience

Guests get instant access to relevant information without having to search or ask staff.

Data and insights

Track which QR codes are scanned most, and use data to optimize future events and understand your guests' behavior.

QR codes for tickets and access

One of the most widespread uses of QR codes for events is digital ticketing. When an attendee purchases a ticket online, they receive a unique QR code via email or in an app. At the entrance, the code is scanned for quick access control.

  1. Generate unique QR codes: Each ticket should have its own unique QR code that links to a ticket ID in your system. This prevents fraud and duplicates.
  2. Test scanning speed: Test your setup with multiple scanners and simulate realistic traffic. A good scanner should handle one scan every 2-3 seconds.
  3. Have a backup plan: Ensure staff can look up tickets manually if the technology fails. Have a list of valid ticket numbers ready.
  4. Use high error correction: Choose error correction level Q or H, as tickets are often displayed on phone screens with varying brightness and screen quality.
  5. Add visual information: Beyond the QR code, the ticket should show the guest's name, event details and a ticket type label (VIP, standard, etc.).

For smaller events where you don't need an advanced ticketing system, you can use QR codes that link to a simple confirmation page or a Google Form for check-in. It's quick to set up and costs nothing.

Calendar QR codes for invitations

Calendar QR codes are perfect for event invitations. When a guest scans the code, the event is automatically added to their calendar with all relevant details — date, time, location and description. This ensures your guests never forget your event.

  • Wedding invitations: Include a QR code on the invitation so guests can save the date, time and venue to their calendar with a single scan.
  • Conference programs: Let attendees scan QR codes at each session to add individual talks to their personal schedule.
  • Corporate events: Share QR codes in internal emails or on posters so colleagues can easily sign up for the holiday party or team building day.

WiFi for event guests

Nothing frustrates event guests more than poor or difficult WiFi access. With a WiFi QR code you give your guests instant access to the network without them having to type long passwords. It takes just one scan, and the phone connects automatically.

Use QRTool.dk's WiFi QR code generator to create the code. Here are the best placements for your WiFi QR code at events:

At the entrance

Place a large WiFi QR code at the registration desk so guests can connect as soon as they arrive.

On the tables

At conferences and dinners, a small WiFi QR code on the table ensures everyone stays connected throughout the event.

In the program

Print the WiFi QR code in the physical program or information materials that attendees receive.

On the big screen

Display the QR code on a big screen between sessions or during breaks so people can easily scan it from their seat.

Always create a dedicated guest network that is separated from the event's internal network. This protects your infrastructure and gives you the ability to control bandwidth for guests.

Feedback after events

Feedback is invaluable for any event organizer, but many guests forget to fill out evaluation forms when they get home. The solution is to collect feedback while guests are still present — and this is where QR codes truly show their value.

Create a QR code that links directly to a feedback form (e.g., Google Forms, Typeform or Microsoft Forms). Place the QR code strategically:

  • At the exit: Guests scan the code on the way out while the experience is still fresh in their memory.
  • On tables at the reception: Give guests the opportunity to provide feedback over a closing drink.
  • In the thank-you email: Send the QR code digitally the day after the event as a supplement to the physical code.
  • On screens: Display the QR code with the text "Tell us how it was" on the event's screens during the last few minutes.

Keep your feedback form short — 3 to 5 questions is ideal. The easier it is to complete, the more responses you'll get. You can optionally offer a small incentive such as a discount code for the next event for those who complete the form.

Social media and networking

Events are about meeting people and creating connections. QR codes can accelerate networking and increase your social media presence during and after your event.

Here are the most effective ways to use QR codes for social networking at events:

  1. Event hashtag and social media profile: Create a QR code that links to your event's Instagram, LinkedIn or Facebook page. Place it visually at photo walls and selfie stations.
  2. Digital business cards with vCard: Encourage attendees to create a vCard QR code with their contact details. When two people meet, they scan each other's code instead of exchanging paper business cards.
  3. LinkedIn profiles: Generate QR codes that link directly to attendees' LinkedIn profiles. Print them on name badges for easy connection.
  4. Photo albums and sharing: Create a QR code for a shared photo album (e.g., Google Photos or iCloud) where all guests can upload and view photos from the event.
  5. App downloads: If your event has a dedicated app, make it easy to download with a QR code that links directly to the App Store or Google Play.

Make sure QR codes for social media are clear and have a call to action (CTA) like "Follow us" or "Share your experience". Context is crucial — guests need to know what happens when they scan the code.

Practical tips for event QR codes

To get the most out of QR codes at your event, it's important to consider the practical aspects. Here are our best tips based on experience from many different types of events.

Size and distance

The rule of thumb is that the QR code should be at least 1/10 of the scanning distance. For a poster scanned from 2 meters, the code should be at least 20 cm. For table cards, 3-4 cm is sufficient.

Contrast and colors

Always use dark modules on a light background. Black on white provides the best scannability. Avoid using your event's brand colors if they have low contrast.

Test in realistic conditions

Test your QR codes in the actual event venue with the lighting that will be present during the event. Blacklights, spotlights and dark venues can affect scannability.

Add a CTA

Always place text next to the QR code explaining what it does: "Scan for program", "Scan for WiFi", "Give feedback". Never a QR code without context.

Additional tips for professional events:

  • Choose SVG format for print: SVG files are vector-based and can be scaled to any size without losing quality — perfect for large banners and posters.
  • Use high error correction: Choose error correction Q (25%) or H (30%) for QR codes printed on materials that may get crumpled, bent or partially covered.
  • Have a central page: Instead of many individual QR codes, you can create a single QR code that links to a mobile-friendly landing page with all event links gathered.
  • Laminate your QR codes: Outdoor events and events with drinks nearby require laminated QR codes to ensure durability.
  • Prepare an offline backup: Ensure critical information (like the program) is also available offline in case of poor mobile coverage.

Frequently asked questions about QR codes for events

How many QR codes do I need for an event?

It depends on your event's size and needs. At a minimum, we recommend three: one for WiFi, one for the program and one for feedback. Larger events may have individual QR codes for each session, sponsor and activity. With QRTool.dk you can generate many QR codes at once.

Can QR codes be used for access control?

Yes, QR codes are well suited for access control. Each ticket is generated with a unique QR code that is scanned at the entrance. For professional use, we recommend linking the QR code to a database system that can mark tickets as used and prevent reuse.

What is the best size for event QR codes?

The size depends on the scanning distance. For table cards and name badges, 3-4 cm is fine. For posters and roll-ups, the code should be at least 15-20 cm. For big screens and banners, it should be 30 cm or more. Always remember a white margin around the code.

Do QR codes work without an internet connection?

The scanning of the QR code itself works offline, but if the code links to a webpage, it requires internet. WiFi QR codes and vCard QR codes work without internet, however, as data is encoded directly in the QR code. Calendar QR codes also work offline.

Can I customize the QR code design to match my event's branding?

Yes, with QRTool.dk you can customize colors, add your logo and choose between different styles (squares, rounded, dots). Just make sure to maintain sufficient contrast between foreground and background so the QR code remains easy to scan.
