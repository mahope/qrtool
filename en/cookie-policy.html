Cookie Policy

Updated 20 November 2026.

QR Tool itself does not set any cookies in your browser. Not to remember your settings and not for statistics. Google does set cookies on the site itself when it shows the consent dialog and ads, though: some are kept even if you say no, others only if you consent. See Advertisements.

What we keep in your browser

  • Settings: theme (light or dark), font size, high contrast and whether you have closed the introduction. They live in your browser's localStorage, not in cookies, and never leave your device.
  • QR history: off by default, and only saved to localStorage if you tick the box yourself. Read more in the privacy policy.
  • Offline copy: the site keeps its own files in the browser so it can open quickly again.

Statistics

We use Plausible and Umami for anonymous visitor statistics. Both are self-hosted by Mahope on our own servers in the EU, use no cookies and store no personal data or anything in your browser. The statistics count page views and clicks on links to other sites. What you type in and what you scan is not sent to the statistics.

Advertisements

QR Tool uses Google AdSense for ads. For that reason the pages load a script from Google (pagead2.googlesyndication.com). No ads are shown until they are switched on, but the script is already loaded so that Google can show the consent dialog.

If you are in the EEA, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, Google shows a consent dialog (Google's "Privacy & messaging") the first time you visit. There you choose whether Google and its partners may use cookies and similar technologies for ads and measurement. If you say no, only ads that do not rely on your data can be shown. You can change your choice later through the button or link that Google's dialog shows on the page, or by deleting the site data for qrtool.dk in your browser.

Cookies from Google's consent dialog and ads: FCCDCF and FCNEC (store your consent choice, also if you say no) and __gads, __gpi and __eoi (ads and measurement, only if you consent). Google decides how long they last. For __gads Google states 13 months; for the others Google does not state an expiry. Google sets them on qrtool.dk. See Google's own list of cookies at business.safety.google/privacy.

Google is responsible for the data it collects for ads. Read more in Google's explanation of how it uses data from ads and Google's privacy policy.

What you type into a QR code is not sent to Google and does not appear in the page address.

How to delete it

Delete the site data for qrtool.dk in your browser's settings and everything QR Tool has stored on your device is gone.
