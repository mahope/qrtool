What is a QR code?

A QR code (Quick Response code) is a two-dimensional barcode that can be scanned with a camera on a smartphone or tablet. QR codes were originally developed in 1994 by the Japanese company Denso Wave for use in the automotive industry, but have today become a universal way to share information.

Unlike traditional barcodes, QR codes can contain much more data - up to 4,296 alphanumeric characters. This makes them ideal for sharing:

  • URLs to websites and landing pages
  • WiFi login credentials
  • Contact information (vCard)
  • Email addresses and messages
  • SMS messages
  • Calendar events
  • Payment information
  • App download links

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is QR Tool free to use?

Yes, QR Tool is 100% free to use. You can generate an unlimited number of QR codes without registration or payment. All features, including logo upload, batch generation and specialized QR types, are freely available.

Do my QR codes expire?

No, QR codes generated with QR Tool never expire. When you download a QR code, it is static and will work forever. However, note that if the QR code links to a URL, that URL must still be active for the QR code to work.

Which format should I choose for printing?

For printing, we recommend SVG format as it is vector-based and can be scaled to any size without loss of quality. Alternatively, you can use PNG at high resolution (1024px or 2048px). Avoid JPG for printing as the compression can make the QR code difficult to scan.

Can I customize the colors of my QR code?

Yes! You can choose any color for both the QR code and the background. We recommend high contrast (e.g., black on white) for best scannability. You can also choose a transparent background for PNG and SVG formats.

How do WiFi QR codes work?

WiFi QR codes contain your network name (SSID), password and encryption type. When someone scans the QR code with their smartphone, the phone automatically connects to the WiFi network without manual entry. This works on most modern Android and iOS devices.

What is error correction and which should I choose?

Error correction determines how much of the QR code can be damaged and still be scannable. L (~7%) is fastest to scan, M (~15%) is standard, Q (~25%) is good for printing, and H (~30%) should be used when adding a logo or when the QR code will be used outdoors.

Can I track how many people scan my QR code?

QR codes generated with QR Tool are static and do not include tracking. To track scans, you should use a URL shortener service like bit.ly or Google Analytics campaign URLs, and then create a QR code for the shortened URL.

How large should my QR code be?

It depends on the scanning distance. As a rule of thumb: the QR code should be at least 1/10 of the scanning distance. This means if people scan from 1 meter away, the QR code should be at least 10cm. For business cards, 2-3cm is often sufficient.

Tips for effective QR codes

Do

  • Test the QR code on multiple devices
  • Use high contrast (black on white is best)
  • Give the QR code enough white space around it
  • Use higher error correction for outdoor use
  • Add a "Scan me" text for context

Avoid

  • Low contrast colors (gray on white)
  • Making the QR code too small
  • Inverting colors (white on black)
  • Placing on curved surfaces
  • JPG format with high compression
